\section*{Reflections on Teaching}

\subsection*{Teaching Philosophy}
In my teaching, the \textit{Problem Based Learning} model engages students with real-world problems and guides them to develop solutions that combine theory with practice.
I have found that students learn best when they see why a topic matters and when they take ownership of their learning, and this shapes how I design courses and supervise projects.

\subsection*{Course Development}
For the \textit{AI Systems \& Infrastructure} course, I prepared tailor-made materials from scratch, writing one piece of blog-style literature for each module.
I designed the materials knowing that most students have little prior knowledge of the underlying systems, so they convey the ideas in a relatable and intuitive way.
Blog-style literature is easy to keep up to date. It is also accessible and flexible, so I can include multimedia content to suit students' different learning styles.

\vspace{\entryskip}
Each module pairs a concrete problem that practitioners face with hands-on exercises, and the exercises build on one another.
The course ends with a mini project in which students combine all the skills learned in the course.
The lectures stay inspirational and serve as an entry point to deeper study of each topic.
The oral exam checks whether students can solve a real-world problem with confidence, which takes more than reciting coding syntax or naming concepts.

\vspace{\entryskip}
The official semester report on the course was very positive. Students especially praised the literature, which they found to be very helpful.

\subsection*{Supervision Approach}
I supervise Bachelor's and Master's semester projects, and I write project proposals that are visually engaging and set a clear direction defined by a practical problem.
My supervision follows the same \textit{Problem Based Learning} model.
I start students with practical problems and real-world examples, focus on the key considerations behind a well-planned project, and give students enough resources to work out how to apply those considerations themselves.

\vspace{\entryskip}
I try to be accessible and supportive while leaving students room to make their own decisions.
I offer guidance on technical approaches and help when they meet obstacles, and I encourage them to take ownership of the direction of their project. I have watched their confidence grow as they take on challenges on their own.
Students have described my supervision as helpful and accessible.

\subsection*{Teaching Plan}
I am satisfied with these practices and intend to keep revising them over time.
I will keep the three core principles and the specific practices I have found effective: blog-style literature, inspirational and engaging lectures, transparency about course and exam formats and expectations, and a progressive arrangement of modules.
I am confident that I can teach a variety of computer science courses well with this approach, and I am interested in developing new courses around topics related to computer science, such as data mining and AI.

\vspace{\entryskip}
I am also prepared to supervise at the PhD level, carrying over the practices that matter for computer science engineering in general: a clear definition of the problem, a clear path to a convincing conclusion, critical thinking about design choices, and quality control in the presentation.
Through research-based teaching and engaged supervision, I aim to produce graduates who think critically about technology and apply their knowledge to real-world problems.
